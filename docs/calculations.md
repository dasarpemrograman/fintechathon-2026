# Calculation and evidence policy

All arithmetic uses Python Decimal with a 50-digit calculation context. Source amounts remain original decimal strings and PostgreSQL NUMERIC without fixed scale. Derived displayed amounts round half up to two decimal places; this is a reporting policy, not a claim about bank settlement precision. FX observations state `1 base = rate quote`; reverse conversion divides. A reference FX rate does not establish executed FX or spendable currency.

Seller contribution is buyer invoice revenue less included reimbursement of buyer-borne advances, seller-borne economic expenses, and financing fees/interest. Loan principal is cash funding and repayment, not revenue or operating expense. Recoverable taxes and reimbursable advances remain separate from seller expenses. An unknown buyer charge prevents a complete buyer total while preserving known seller expenses and contribution.

Cost components cannot overlap their quoted coverage. Actual amounts replace estimates for the same component. For a sales-dependent expense fraction `s`, target contribution margin `m`, fixed expenses `C` and financing expense `F`, floor revenue is `(C + F) / (1 - m - s)`; `m + s >= 1` is invalid. A single-line floor is divided by its declared quantity before comparison with destination prices. Retail/PPP data, wrong product/form/grade/use, destination, expiry, unsupported units, duplicate sources and unmet MOQ are excluded with reasons. A floor above observed prices remains an unresolved economic conflict, with no forced recommendation.

Freight primitives use quoted rules: sea LCL chargeable W/M is max(cubic metres, gross kilograms / 1000); air uses max(gross kilograms, cubic centimetres / quoted divisor); FCL uses declared container quantity. Charge is max(quoted minimum, chargeable units × rate). The independent W/M regression is 2000 kg, 3 m³, USD100/W/M, USD350 minimum → USD350. Quote/input integration and broader provider coverage remain tracked in OpenSpec.

Cash is opening available funds less commitments elsewhere plus chronological obligation and financing events. Economic allocation of prior-paid inventory or overhead creates no second cash payment. Unknown material cost/timing and uncovered invoice balances remain explicit partial inputs. Same-instant events without complete sequence use debit-first stress ordering. Negative intervals and loan repayment extend through the full declared liability horizon. Local dates remain local; missing intraday timing is never fabricated from a value date.

Moving the draw after day-14 payments leaves a minimum of -IDR46m. Tests define expectations independently from the calculator; fixture source schedules are in `fixtures/demo/`.

Every newly calculated analysis includes formula descriptions and exact input/evidence revision references. `GET /v2/inputs/{id}/revisions/{revision}` retrieves authorized historical evidence and numeric inputs; original locators, content hashes and extraction text remain available. Scenario patches and invoice facts are also retained through scenario and workspace/deal revision APIs and export. An old analysis never silently follows a newly edited source.

Cash minima round down and nonnegative funding requirements round up to the displayed cent, so a sub-cent deficit cannot disappear into a readiness check. Other displayed amounts use decimal half-up rounding; original native inputs retain their source precision. FX conversion is a valuation and does not prove that spendable funds exist in the payment currency.

Producer batches retain input quantities/unit costs, prior-paid inventory treatment, labor/process/overhead costs, declared capacity and usable yield. Yield is greater than zero and at most one. Capacity must cover the batch; batch quantity times usable yield must cover finished export quantities in comparable units. Unknown or incompatible units cannot establish execution readiness. Prior-paid inventory and allocated overhead remain economic costs without creating another cash payment. Buyer stress changes only unsettled forecast portions, preserves verified receipt history and fixed supplier obligations, and reports explicit exposure without assigning a default probability. Cross-currency stress is a reference valuation, not proof of actual currency availability.

Execution also checks that seller-paid cash costs are covered by linked obligations in their native currency. A Decimal allocation flow prevents one all-in liability from being reused to cover several expenses twice. Increased actual charges require corresponding liabilities; settling an older, lower invoice does not make the new cost paid. Reference FX cannot manufacture native-currency liability coverage. Paid-before-snapshot costs remain economic expenses without a second projected cash payment.

Scenario comparisons retain their own immutable input revisions and assess the original owner's margin, cash buffer, permitted change scope, delivery deadline and credit-tenor constraints. Missing shipment/delivery anchors remain unknown. Relative ranks use a disclosed lexicographic order of constraint violations/unknowns, then cash shortfall, margin and unpaid receivable exposure according to the selected preference; no default probability or weighted score is fabricated. Ranking never applies terms, accepts them on behalf of a counterparty or authorizes execution. Buyer/supplier/production changes can be recalculated together through the same engine.

Finance product screening compares reviewed jurisdiction, entity, instrument, stage, native currency, amount, tenor, security, documents and pricing. Missing rates and unsupported rate bases remain unknown. Normal-mode screening of manually recorded products makes no provider call and consumes no enriched run. Application packs bind a scenario/analysis, requested terms, native-currency funds-use schedule and provider/recommended checklists. Application-ready describes preparation; it does not submit an application, approve a loan or verify a disbursement.

Explicit `withheld_fee` events reduce a matching same-instant native-currency draw before it enters the cash timeline. Interest and separately paid financing costs remain outflows; principal does not enter contribution. Assigned-receivable scenarios suppress only the assigned uncollected portion of the buyer cash event. Gross advance plus gross reserve must equal assigned face value. Reserve release follows modeled buyer collection plus the declared delay, so a late buyer cannot release it early. Duplicate assignment, a second principal repayment against the same assigned collection, overlarge deductions and unexplained settlement balances fail validation.

Agreed bank-fee deductions preserve the nominal obligation and append a deduction plus a seller-borne actual expense. They do not create a second cash outflow. Reimbursement adjustments create separately evidenced buyer claims against an actual reimbursable advance; existing invoice recoveries and prior claims cap the amount so recovery is never duplicated or treated as sales revenue. A write-off appends reviewed loss evidence, removes future cash and reduces buyer receivable contribution; `resolved_with_writeoff` is distinct from `paid`. Supplier write-offs require a matching reviewed final cost to pass native liability coverage and closure.

Approved write-off amounts remain fixed across receipt correction. Reversing a previous payment reopens only the balance not covered by an existing reviewed write-off; it does not silently increase the loss. Closure checks remaining verified allocations and explicit write-off amounts, rather than trusting a historic status flag. Contribution lineage includes the obligation, receipt and conversion revisions used for losses and realized receivable FX differences.
